🌲 The Paper Industry & Deforestation

The pulp and paper industry is the fourth largest industrial consumer of energy in the world and contributes to roughly 13–15% of total wood harvested globally. While some of that wood comes from sustainably managed plantations, a significant portion comes from natural forests — including tropical forests in Asia, South America, and Africa.

In Southeast Asia and South Asia — the region closest to LowCostPDF users — deforestation linked to pulp production has degraded critical habitats, increased flooding risk, and contributed to the loss of biodiversity in countries like Indonesia, Myanmar, and parts of northeastern India. Bangladesh itself has seen its Sundarbans mangrove forest — the world's largest — face increasing pressure.

Sources: FAO Global Forest Resources Assessment; Environmental Paper Network; WWF Forest Practice Reports.

📉 Paper Use Trends & What They Mean

1990s
Paper consumption peaks in developed countries. Office and printing paper dominate. Global paper output nearly doubles between 1980 and 2000.
2000s
Digital documents begin reducing office paper. However, Asia's paper consumption grows rapidly as economies expand and literacy rates rise.
2010s
Smartphones and tablets slow paper growth in some sectors. Education and printing-on-demand still consume massive quantities in South Asia.
2020s
Global push for sustainability gains momentum. Tools like LowCostPDF empower individuals to immediately reduce consumption without going fully paperless.
