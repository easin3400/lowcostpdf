🌍 Why Paper Use Matters

Paper might seem harmless — it's just wood pulp, right? But the global paper industry is one of the leading causes of deforestation, water pollution, and greenhouse gas emissions. Every time you print a full-page PDF when you only needed to read it once, a small piece of a forest pays the price.

The good news: you have direct control over this. By simply printing fewer sheets — using a multi-page layout — you can meaningfully reduce your personal paper footprint without changing your lifestyle at all.

"The single most effective thing an individual can do to reduce paper consumption is to print multiple pages per sheet whenever possible."

🌲 How Your Printing Connects to Forests

Roughly 8–10 litres of water and a significant fraction of a full-grown tree go into producing every 500 sheets of office paper (a standard ream). A single tree yields roughly 8,333 sheets. That sounds like a lot — until you consider that a billion reams of paper are produced globally every year.

Forests absorb CO₂, regulate rainfall, prevent soil erosion, and are home to over 80% of the world's land species. When we consume less paper, we slow the demand that drives logging — both legal and illegal. In countries like Bangladesh, where coastal forests and hill-tract ecosystems are already under pressure, every small act of conservation counts.
